    Generative Models and Diffusion¶

    Introduction¶

    A generative model learns patterns in data and can produce new samples that resemble those patterns. Language models generate token sequences; image-generation systems can generate or edit images; audio and video systems operate on their supported media representations. Generation is probabilistic and does not guarantee factuality, originality, or permission to use an output.

    Families of generative models¶

    Family Broad idea Example applications
    Autoregressive models Predict the next element conditioned on previous elements Text, code, image or audio token generation
    Variational autoencoders (VAEs) Learn a compressed latent representation and decode samples from it Image reconstruction and latent representations
    Generative adversarial networks (GANs) Train a generator against a discriminator in a competitive setup Image synthesis and domain-specific generation
    Diffusion models Learn to reverse a gradual noising process Image, audio, and video generation
    Flow-based models Learn an invertible mapping between a simple distribution and data Density estimation and sample generation

    This table is an orientation, not a ranking. Modern systems may combine families or use tokenized representations of images, audio, or video.

    How diffusion generation works¶

    During a simplified training process, a clean example is progressively corrupted with noise. A neural network learns to estimate or remove noise at different steps. During generation, the system starts from noise and iteratively applies learned denoising steps to form a sample.

    Text-to-image systems commonly encode a prompt, condition generation on that representation, and decode a generated latent into an image. Some systems use latent diffusion, applying denoising in a compressed latent space rather than directly over every image pixel. This can reduce computation, though exact designs differ.

    What controls generation?¶

    • Prompt or conditioning input: describes the desired content or style.
    • Seed: may make a particular sampling path reproducible for a given implementation and settings; it does not make systems identical across versions or hardware.
    • Guidance scale: controls how strongly generation follows some conditioning mechanisms; excessive guidance can reduce quality.
    • Steps or sampler: influence the iterative generation process and its latency-quality trade-off.
    • Reference inputs and masks: support editing, inpainting, or image-to-image workflows where offered.

    The names and effects of controls vary by model and product. Validate results with the target task rather than assuming a setting has universal behavior.

    Real-world applications¶

    Creative teams may use image generation for ideation, storyboards, or draft assets. Product teams may generate controlled illustrations or personalized imagery. Speech and video systems can help create or transform media. In each case, users need a clear distinction between generated, edited, and captured content.

    Developer perspective and safeguards¶

    Build a media workflow that checks file type and size, scans uploads, limits output dimensions and duration, stores provenance metadata, and provides a human review path. Address licensing, consent, impersonation, content moderation, and applicable disclosure requirements. Do not imply that a generated image is a photograph or that the system can establish who created a source image.

    Common misconceptions¶

    • Diffusion is not simply copying a nearest training image.
    • A fixed seed does not guarantee identical results in every environment.
    • A generated image is not evidence that the depicted event occurred.
    • A polished sample does not establish that the model is safe for every group or commercial use.
