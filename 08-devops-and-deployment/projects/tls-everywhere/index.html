    TLS Everywhere¶

    Serve TaskFlow over HTTPS with a Let's Encrypt certificate that renews itself, redirect all HTTP to HTTPS, add HSTS, alert before a certificate can expire, and practice diagnosing a broken certificate chain with openssl s_client.

    Why this project helps¶

    • Builds working knowledge of certificates, ACME, renewal, and TLS termination, which every public service needs.
    • Improves security: encrypted traffic everywhere, with HSTS preventing downgrade to plain HTTP.
    • Improves reliability: expired certificates cause real outages, and automated renewal with alerts prevents them.
    • "The site shows a certificate error for some users" is a common incident and interview scenario.

    What you need¶

    • A server with a public IP and a domain pointing at it; see Production Server from Scratch and Your Own Domain, Properly.
    • Nginx with certbot, or Caddy (which handles certificates automatically).
    • openssl and curl on your laptop; an uptime or monitoring tool for the expiry alert.

    How to do it¶

    1. Read TLS and certificate fundamentals for chains, SNI, and ACME challenges.
    2. Request a certificate against Let's Encrypt's staging environment first (certbot --staging, or Caddy's acme_ca setting), and switch to production once it works; see obtaining and renewing certificates.
    3. Configure Nginx (or Caddy) to terminate TLS for TaskFlow and redirect port 80 to HTTPS; see TLS termination and troubleshooting.
    4. Add a Strict-Transport-Security header, starting with a short max-age and raising it once everything works.
    5. Prove renewal works with certbot renew --dry-run (Caddy renews on its own), and check that the renewal timer is active.
    6. Add an expiry alert from your monitoring, because Let's Encrypt no longer sends expiry emails and certificate lifetimes are getting shorter.
    7. Break the chain on purpose (serve only the leaf certificate without the intermediate) and diagnose it with openssl s_client, passing -servername for SNI and -showcerts to see what the server sends.
    8. Fix the chain and confirm with curl -v and an online TLS checker.

    Done when¶

    • http:// requests redirect to https:// with a permanent redirect.
    • Responses carry an HSTS header.
    • certbot renew --dry-run succeeds (or Caddy shows automatic renewal).
    • An alert fires in a test when a certificate is within your warning window.
    • You can explain the broken-chain error from the openssl s_client output and show the fix.

    Stretch goals¶

    • Re-encrypt traffic from Nginx to the app on the internal hop.
    • Require client certificates (mTLS) for an internal admin endpoint.
    • Test with a certificate from a second ACME CA.
